# Privacy — independent player-history service

Updated October 29, 2026. Controls for deletion, stopping further collection and clearing older diagnostics are active and have been checked. A documented legitimate-interest assessment now covers community progress history. Full controller identification and effective information for players remain unresolved; some provider and historical-copy questions also remain open. This notice describes current data handling; the remaining assessments are not a certification of compliance.

Project contact: **Shuriel**, a private operator based in Poland · [contact@shuriel.com](mailto:contact@shuriel.com). Full identification of the person responsible for this service remains to be completed; the project name alone does not provide it.

## What the service does

The service obtains public player data from Rhythia's official API and keeps profile statistics and selected score observations for progress and comparisons. Full histories are served to Reimagined installations through an anonymous access key; requests without one are limited (see Public access). Collection runs independently of extension users. The service is free and noncommercial.

Rhythia controls the original game records. The operator determines the purposes and handling of this separate copy. Public data can still relate to an identifiable person. Capo Games' acceptance of the described API use does not authorize every future use or replace data-protection responsibilities.

## Data and purposes

| Data | Purpose and availability |
| --- | --- |
| Public player ID and last known name, country and avatar | Identify profiles. Public with history; no intentional archive of name, country or avatar changes. |
| Daily statistics and ranks | Progress/comparisons: skill points, global/country rank, play count, squares hit, average accuracy, total scores and average rhythm points where available. Public in an individual player's history. |
| Play time and account age | The total play time in Standard, VR and Spin as Rhythia reports it, once a day for each collected profile, and the date the account was created, once. Used privately for community statistics, such as how many hours the Top 5000 played in a month or the oldest and newest accounts. No API route serves it. |
| Latest observed ranking position and dates last seen within Top 1000, 3000 and 5000 | Privately choose which profiles to collect and how often. Observing a ranking entry does not itself make an individual profile history public. |
| Selected Standard-mode scores for active Top-5000 players (Top 1000, or seen in Top 5000 within the last seven days) | Up to five results per actual UTC play date: score/player IDs, play time, map reference/title/difficulty/note count, reported rhythm points, speed, misses, pass status, mods and replay link where supplied. Private; not currently exposed by the public history API. Replay files are not downloaded for this history. |
| Score-selection context and capture records | Privately record observed personal-Top-100 score IDs, source lists, selection reasons, previous retained best-score comparisons, estimated weighted points, capture times, available-window coverage and errors. These are bounded observations, not a complete play or replay archive. |
| Best scores | The personal top-score lists of ranked players, how they change over time and monthly summaries of them, with basic information about the maps in them. Used for progress and comparisons. The lists themselves are private and not exposed by the public history API. The comparison feature shows only group figures (medians and shares) of at least five players ranked near a player, never another player's ID, name or list. |
| Leaderboard list | Once a day, Rhythia's public leaderboard of the top 50,000 players in Standard (Lock) and the top 200 in Spin and VR: player ID, position, rhythm points and country only, without names, profiles or scores. Used privately for counts and summaries, such as players new to or gone from the leaderboard and rank changes. No API route serves it. Players excluded after a granted request are left out of new copies and removed from stored ones. |
| Hourly leaderboard check (trial until November 6, 2026) | Every hour, Rhythia's public Standard leaderboard of the top 5,000: player ID, position, rhythm points and play count, without names, profiles or scores. Kept privately: each player's latest values and, for each hour, only the players whose rhythm points or play count changed. For each profile the daily collection reads during the trial, a private record of those latest values next to what the read brought (rhythm points, play count, squares hit, total scores, average accuracy, the time of the newest play and how many best scores entered or left the list). Used only to test whether reading just the profiles of players who played would keep the same history, and how much less it would ask of Rhythia. No API route serves it. Players excluded after a granted request are left out and removed. |
| Map catalog and map search | Rhythia's public map pages, and its Ranked and Qualified lists read once a day: map ID, title, mapper name and ID, stars, difficulty, length, play count, the date it was uploaded, whether it is marked explicit, status, place in those lists and links to the map art; every hour, changes to the Ranked and Qualified lists (qualified, ranked, vetoed, back to unranked, removed) with the hour they were seen, when a map was qualified and how many vetoes it has, without who vetoed or why; and how each map plays, worked out from its file (type, shares of movement, quantum, notes per second; the notes themselves are kept privately to work this out again). The API answers how listed maps play (type, shares of movement, quantum and notes per second, never the notes themselves) and Reimagined Search, a search of Ranked and Qualified maps by type, quantum, stars, length, mapper and title. A search answers only what a map card shows, the type and quantum, never the shares or notes. Search filters are used only to answer: they are not written to the database or logs, the same search is answered from the API's memory for up to 5 minutes, and a short code of each result page stays in memory until the UTC day ends, to count the key's limit. A few maps are deliberately answered with a different type, partly depending on the access key, so that copies of the map types can be recognised. |
| Community statistics | Occasional summaries of what ranked players play, such as maps, map types, mods and accuracy. Private unless the operator publishes a summary. |
| Last observed game ban status/date and observation time | Privately diagnose availability. No public ban history; a missing profile is not automatically banned. |
| Optional visit reports: viewed player ID, counts and last accepted visit | Discover public profiles. Private; no visitor game account in the report. |
| Connection IP, requested resource, time and response information | Deliver requests, limit abuse and diagnose failures. A short-lived identifier generated from the IP address and viewed profile helps limit repeat reports. Visit-report records do not store the raw IP, but providers and connection logs can still process it. |
| Anonymous access key | Serve full history to extension installations and limit automated copying. The extension asks for a key once it has online history and renews it about every 20 days. A key is random, signed by the service and contains no game account, session or browser information. The service does not store keys; it checks their signature. A key's random ID, never the key itself, can appear in the operator's abuse report and blocklist. Each UTC day the service also counts how many different keys made a valid request (history, map types or map search) and keeps only that number, together with the number of keys issued; no key or key ID is kept for this. |
| History usage counters and temporary blocks | Limit how many different players' full histories, and how many player IDs in leaderboard requests, one key and one address (IPv4, or IPv6 /64) can read per UTC day and per 10 minutes. With a key, a sudden burst or repeated requests after a refusal block that key or address for 15 minutes, doubling on repeats that day up to 4 hours. Without a key, a burst is refused only until the 10-minute window ends, and the answer gives the wait time; requests without a key from that address are blocked only after about 120 refused tries within 10 minutes, then also for 15 minutes, doubling on repeats that day up to 4 hours. Each address block (IPv6 /48, IPv4 /24) also has daily counters: it opens at most 128 budgets and gets at most 25 keys a day, further addresses in it share one budget, and its visit reports past a daily ceiling are not recorded. Leaderboard requests read up to 30 days for at most 2,000 different players per key (4,000 per address) per UTC day; past that they still receive the last seven days. Map-type requests (how listed maps play, from the service's map catalog) are counted in distinct map IDs, at most 1,500 per key and 4,000 per address per UTC day. Map searches need a key and are counted in distinct result pages, at most 300 per key and 900 per address per UTC day, and 60 per key and 180 per address in 10 minutes. Kept in the API's memory only, never written to the database or the connection log. To set future limits, the service also measures, for each key and address, how many different players it read over the last seven UTC days and how many requested player IDs were unknown; nothing is refused on this yet. After the UTC day ends, this measurement holds the key, address and players only as keyed hashes under a random secret that exists only in the API's memory, so a returning key or address is recognised but no longer named. The hourly operational log keeps only totals and spreads, without key IDs, addresses or player IDs. |
| Abuse report and blocklist | Private. For keys and addresses that were blocked, refused for a burst or have used at least 80% of a daily budget, the operator's report shows the key ID, address and counts, so that abuse can be stopped. It also lists address blocks (IPv6 /48, IPv4 /24) that opened unusually many budgets or took unusually many keys that day, with those counts, and keys and addresses with unusual seven-day reading (at least half of a proposed weekly budget, near a daily budget on two or more days, or many unknown player IDs), named only if they made a request that UTC day. Ordinary use is not named in it. The operator can block addresses, ranges or key IDs. |
| Unexpected API responses and diagnostic details | Privately investigate missing/inconsistent statistics. Copies can contain older public values. |
| Requests and exclusions | Handle rights, document completion and prevent recollection. Private. |

The service does not receive your session, password, friends list or extension preferences. Cloud history reveals the requested profile even without visit reports. Players can be collected without using Reimagined.

Collection uses ranking groups and recent profile discovery. The current UTC schedule checks Top 5000 and a bounded group of recently visited profiles daily. Promotion is immediate; leaving a ranking group has a seven-day grace period. Profiles that leave Top 5000 can remain in a less frequent collection group for 60 days, checked on the first and third Sunday; afterwards collection stops until they qualify again. Previously admitted profiles with no ranking observation have a transition period. Stopping routine collection does not automatically erase existing history.

On the last UTC day of the month, the daily collection starts later (22:00 UTC instead of 17:00 UTC), so the month's best-score summaries reflect that day. Monthly Top-25 captures were planned for eligible Top-5000 players; they stopped before the first one was taken, and none were stored. Daily selected-score history covers the same Top-5000 group. API windows, quotas, errors and unavailable profiles can interrupt or limit all of these observations. If the game API reports that a profile is missing, earlier history is kept. This is separate from a request to remove personal data.

The proposed basis is legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR: maintaining community progress records and comparisons using limited public game data. An assessment dated October 18, 2026 documents the purpose, necessity, less intrusive alternatives and effects on players, including younger players, long retention and public access. It supports the stated purpose and bounded data needs, but does not give unconditional clearance for the entire current public service while transparency obligations remain unresolved. How to inform players who do not use Reimagined, or whether an applicable exception can be justified, also remains unresolved. A visitor's opt-in is not the collected player's consent.

## Public access and providers

Individual player histories are available without login or account. Full histories are served to extension installations with an anonymous access key, within daily and 10-minute budgets per key and per address. Since October 29, 2026, requests without a key receive just the last three calendar days, with a smaller per-address budget, and map types, map search and Near your rank need a key. The service does **not offer a bulk database download**. Measures to limit abuse cannot guarantee that recipients will not copy or combine individual histories. Visit reports, IP-related safeguards, ranking-selection records, selected-score records, best-score lists, hourly leaderboard checks, community statistics, private ban observations, diagnostics and records of privacy requests are not public history.

The operator can access records for operation, security and requests. OVHcloud hosts the service; Cloudflare delivers and protects it and stores encrypted offsite recovery copies in an R2 bucket in the EU jurisdiction. OVHcloud email handles new project correspondence; privacy@shuriel.com reaches the same project inbox as contact@shuriel.com. Earlier correspondence remains in Gmail during the transition. Gmail still receives generic new-mail alerts in the operator's own mailboxes; these alerts do not include the original sender, subject, message contents or attachments. Encrypted backups are no longer delivered by email. This is consumer Gmail; Google does not offer a processor agreement for that service. History-service Discord alerts use counts and general causes without player identifiers. Older notifications may still contain earlier details.

The extension sends the requested public player ID, its anonymous access key and normal connection information for history access, and a viewed player ID if its separate visit-report option is enabled. For map types it sends map identifiers (song IDs, map hashes or map names, as Rhythia gives them), and for a map search its filters, with the same key. The service does not receive the extension's local history or game session. Other clients of the public history API also expose their requested player ID and connection information.

Some providers process information outside Poland and the European Economic Area. The hosting location does not mean every provider processes information only in Poland. The active OVHcloud account confirms its processing agreement, and Cloudflare's self-service terms incorporate its DPA. These agreements include provisions for covered processing and international transfers; they do not mean that all processing stays in the EEA. The roles and transfer arrangements for remaining recipients are still being assessed. Published information: [OVHcloud processing agreement](https://contract.eu.ovhapis.com/1.0/pdf/OVH_Data_Protection_Agreement-pl.pdf), [Cloudflare DPA](https://www.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-customer-dpa/), [Google consumer-service information](https://support.google.com/policies/answer/9581826?hl=en) and [Discord](https://discord.com/privacy).

Data is not sold or used for advertising, creditworthiness or lending. Later functions with new purposes/data require a separate assessment and updated notice.

## Automated operational summaries

An isolated AI reviewer receives bounded, filtered source-code changes and extracted facts about changes to Rhythia's public website through OpenRouter to a routed model provider. The configured model is Anthropic Claude Haiku; retained production responses identify Google as the inference provider. Routing may change among endpoints meeting the required data-handling settings. It supports operational review; it does not assess players or make decisions about them. Player histories, score records, visit reports, support correspondence, raw request logs, credentials and game sessions are excluded from its intended inputs. Filtering withholds files that match sensitive-data patterns, but cannot guarantee detection of every possible sensitive value. Earlier one-off evaluations used other models routed through OpenRouter, on sanitized test code only.

Provider routing requires zero data retention and disallows data collection for model training. Input/output logging and external observability broadcasts are disabled in the checked workspace. Request restrictions are separate from account-wide defaults and do not independently verify provider behaviour. OpenRouter publishes a standard DPA through its Terms; its application to this individual noncommercial account is still being clarified. Local review jobs/results normally expire after 14 days; current sanitized source baselines are replaced as source changes. Published administrative summaries and Discord notices have separate retention. Provider information is available from [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr). This does not introduce AI processing of collected player statistics.

## Retention

Operational reports about collection progress, API structure and general failures are sent to administrative Discord channels. They do not contain raw game responses, player identifiers or game sessions. Infrastructure alerts separately describe resource usage, watched project-file metadata and SSH logins; SSH alerts may include the administrative account, source IP address and authentication method. Discord messages have separate provider retention and are not automatically deleted by the history service's 14-day diagnostic cleanup. See [Discord's privacy information](https://discord.com/privacy).

| Records | How long they are kept |
| --- | --- |
| Player history | No automatic expiry while the service is provided. Continued need is reviewed at least annually and when the purpose or scope changes; history is erased when a removal request is granted. |
| Selected daily scores, capture records and the latest personal-Top-100 reference | A six-calendar-month cutoff, using the UTC play/observation date and hourly cleanup. Unreferenced shared map metadata is also removed. |
| Best scores | Six months after a score leaves the list; monthly summaries 24 months; map information without a set end date. Hourly cleanup. |
| Leaderboard list | Daily copies 90 days, Monday copies one year, the copy of each month's first day without a set end date. Daily cleanup. |
| Hourly leaderboard check (trial) | 30 days, cleaned hourly; removed at once for a granted removal request. Reading stops on November 6, 2026. |
| Play time and account creation date | Play time 13 months, with daily cleanup; the account creation date as long as the player record. Both are erased with the player record. |
| Map catalog and map search | Map information and Ranked and Qualified status changes without a set end date; a map's place in the Ranked or Qualified list is cleared when it leaves that list. Search filters are not written to the database or logs; a search stays in the API's memory for up to 5 minutes, and a short code of each result page until the UTC day ends. |
| Community statistics | Collected scores six months; saved summaries are reviewed with the yearly assessment. |
| Ranking-selection records | Until their last Top-5000 observation is more than 365 days old, with hourly cleanup; also erased for a granted removal request. |
| Last private ban observation | Kept with the player record and removed when that record is erased. |
| Visit counts and last-visit observations | Counts cover the current and previous 13 days, measured in UTC; last-visit observations are kept for 14 days. Automatic cleanup applies to these records, separately from provider and connection logs. |
| Identifier used to limit repeat visit reports | Used for a rolling hour, then normally removed within the following hour. |
| History usage counters, temporary blocks and the abuse report | In the API's memory until 00:00 UTC or a restart; a block lasts at most 4 hours. The seven-day measurement keeps hashed counts for at most seven UTC days, also in memory only and cleared by a restart. The report is computed from these counters when the operator opens it and is not stored. |
| Access keys | Not stored by the service. A key stops working 30 days after it was issued; the extension removes its copy when online history is turned off. |
| Daily key counts | Two numbers per UTC day (different keys that made a valid request, keys issued), with no key, key ID or address. Kept as aggregate statistics without a set end date. |
| Blocklist entries | Only while needed to stop the abuse they were added for; reviewed at least monthly. |
| Identifiable diagnostic responses/files and detailed collection logs | 14 days, including details marked to keep visible. Summary alerts omit player IDs. This period applies to the service's diagnostic records, not every infrastructure log. |
| Request correspondence | As needed to handle the request, then at most 90 days after actual closure. Verification material is removed sooner when no longer needed. This policy must also be applied manually in the mailbox. |
| Minimal completion record | Case number, player ID, dates, scope and result: 12 months after closure. |
| Record preventing further collection | Only the player ID, exclusion date and case number, while needed to prevent collection from starting again. This is kept separately from the completion record. |

A specific dispute or legal requirement can justify a limited, documented and reviewed exception. An open case is not a reason to keep correspondence forever. Since October 20, 2026, the service's own connection log keeps only a shortened address (the first three parts of an IPv4 address, or the first three groups of an IPv6 address), the request without the viewed player ID, the response code, size and time; no browser or referring page. It is rotated by size (about 30 MB) rather than after a fixed number of days. Retention of records held by providers remains unresolved separately; the service's 14-day diagnostic period does not cover every infrastructure log.

New routine backups omit visit reports and diagnostic responses. They keep profile history, retained score/monthly/ranking records and the information needed to respect requests to stop collection. Local routine rotation keeps two daily copies, two copies from the every-three-days schedule, three weekly copies and three monthly copies. Named encrypted manual daily copies expire after 31 days; named deployment database recovery copies after 180 days. This narrow automatic rule does not include unknown archives, frozen research material or source/log archives.

Encrypted recovery copies are also uploaded to a Cloudflare R2 bucket in the EU jurisdiction. Verified R2 lifecycle rules expire daily copies after 31 days, weekly after 90 days, and monthly and legacy history copies after 180 days. A 30-day bucket lock prevents earlier deletion; lifecycle processing is asynchronous, so these are configured expiry periods rather than an exact deletion timestamp. Until the switch-off on October 25, 2026 at 12:00 UTC, weekly/monthly encrypted copies were also delivered to the operator's Gmail accounts; copies already mailed remain there until removed. Mailed backup retention is checked manually: 31 days for daily, 90 for weekly and 180 for monthly copies. Local rotation does not delete R2 objects or email attachments. **Unclassified recovery archives, older project copies and earlier correspondence still require separate review.** Encryption protects these copies but does not erase them. If a backup is recovered, current removal requests and cleanup must be applied before records become available again. Copies held by other tools or browsers cannot be remotely recalled.

## Requests and contact

Email [privacy@shuriel.com](mailto:privacy@shuriel.com) with a profile link/ID and the request. You can ask about access, correction, erasure, restriction or objection, as applicable. You do not need to delete the original account first. Contact Rhythia separately for its records.

A public link alone does not prove account control. When there is reasonable doubt, proportionate verification is arranged privately. Screen sharing is not mandatory and is not recorded by the operator. Do not send passwords, tokens, recovery codes, identity documents or unrelated personal information.

The erasure process first stops further collection, then removes the service's active player, profile history, selected-score/monthly records, best scores, the player's community statistics, ranking-selection records and diagnostics and records the result. If cleanup is incomplete, the request remains unfinished. A later verified return request starts new history from zero. Blocking a profile because of an operational problem is separate and does not itself erase history. History responses ask browsers and other clients not to keep temporary cached copies. Previously cached responses and other copies are assessed separately; this cannot remotely delete copies already obtained by others.

For connection/visit requests, provide information that helps locate records. Reports do not include your account and cannot necessarily be found by nickname. We do not collect additional identity information solely to identify otherwise unidentifiable records.

Requests are handled without undue delay, normally within one month. A permitted extension is explained within that first month; refusals include reasons and complaint information. You can complain to a data-protection authority, including [UODO](https://uodo.gov.pl/). Not every right applies to every basis; public API access is not a substitute for assessing a request.

The notice will be updated when the remaining assessments are completed. For data used in your browser see [extension Privacy](PRIVACY.md); for service use see [history Terms](HISTORY-TERMS.md).
